# Spectral survey of the radio corpus (2026-10-26)

`scripts/corpus/spectral_survey.py ~/calls --out ~/reports_spectral`: 58 raw scanner recordings
(feeds 4390, 4395, 4430), 35.8 minutes. Every 16 ms a 32 ms frame is labelled from its FFT
(the rules are in the script's header); spectrograms with the label strip are in `~/reports_spectral/png/`,
per-frame features in `~/reports_spectral/frames/`, the mean spectrum per label in `spectra.csv`.

## What the channel is

- **Brick wall at 4.6 kHz.** Every file has energy up to about 4.6 kHz and nothing above: the mean speech
  spectrum drops from -38 dB at 4 kHz to -104 dB at 5 kHz. The feed is
  band-limited by the radio system and the stream codec, not by the microphones. Anything the app expects
  above 4.6 kHz (fricatives s/f/th live at 4-8 kHz in natural speech) is simply not there.
- **Bass-heavy speech.** During speech the frequency below which 95 % of the energy lies is
  1594 Hz (median per file) and the spectral centroid 665 Hz.
  The mean speech spectrum peaks at 500 Hz and is already 10 dB down by 1 kHz and
  24 dB down by 3 kHz.
- **Levels.** Speech sits at -28 dBFS (median per file), the carrier-open floor at
  -48 dBFS: an in-channel SNR of about 20 dB. Squelch-closed gaps are digital
  silence (below -90 dBFS), so "silence" in the corpus is mostly the channel being closed, not room noise.
- **No mains hum** in the corpus (0.0 % of frames): the 60 Hz problem is only on the Jetson's USB mic.

## What is in the frames

| label | share of frames (mean per file) |
|---|---|
| speech | 58.7 % |
| silence | 28.2 % |
| other | 11.5 % |
| tone | 1.4 % |
| noise | 0.1 % |
| hum | 0.1 % |

- **Speech** is the bulk of every open carrier. "Other" is mostly unvoiced speech (consonants, breaths) and
  the first frames after a key-up.
- **Tones** are short. No file has a tone that holds still for 0.25 s or longer: there are no sustained
  sirens in these 58 calls, only brief alert/squelch beeps (the 1 kHz radio beep of CS010 and CS013 is one)
  and the odd vowel with one dominant harmonic. The files with the most tone frames are
  mocomdps-4390-1792804749 (6.9 %), 4395-1792803166 (6.5 %) and 4430-1792827672 (6.5 %).
- **Broadband noise** (static, engine, wind) is almost absent as separate frames: it is under the speech,
  not between it. The lowest-SNR files are mocomdps-4390-1792791761 (11 dB), 4430-1792827672 (15 dB) and
  4390-1792805285 (15 dB); listen to those for what "scratchy" is.

## How the synthetic voice compares

Piper's en_US-lessac-medium voice reading the STEMI script is darker than the radio: its 95 % roll-off is
719 Hz against the corpus' 1517 Hz, and it is full-band to 11 kHz. `scripts/corpus/radioize.py` equalises
a WAV to the corpus' mean speech spectrum (third-octave smoothed, +-24 dB), brick-walls it at 4.6 kHz,
soft-limits it and adds a -48 dBFS noise bed shaped like the carrier-open floor. On the STEMI script the
EQ is about -7 dB at 250 Hz, +3 dB at 2 kHz and -23 dB at 4 kHz; the result's roll-off is 1688 Hz. The
air test plays both versions through the speaker and mic (AIR_SCRIPTS_2026-10-26.md).

## Towards a cloned radio voice

What radioize.py does is the channel, not the person. A voice that sounds like the medics themselves needs
a speaker-conditioned TTS (XTTS-v2, F5-TTS or OpenVoice take a 6-15 s clean reference clip); on the Jetson
that is CPU-only and slow but workable offline, and the corpus has single-speaker bursts long enough to use
as references (S025_01 of CS008 is 84 s of one paramedic). Steps: pick 3-4 medics with clean bursts, cut
6-15 s references, synthesise the practice scripts, then radioize the result so the channel matches too.
