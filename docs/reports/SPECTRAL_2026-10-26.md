# Spectral survey of the radio corpus (2026-10-26)

`scripts/corpus/spectral_survey.py ~/calls --out ~/reports_spectral`: 58 raw scanner recordings
(feeds 4390, 4395, 4430), 35.8 minutes. Every 16 ms a 32 ms frame is labelled from its FFT
(the rules are in the script's header); spectrograms with the label strip are in `~/reports_spectral/png/`,
per-frame features in `~/reports_spectral/frames/`, the mean spectrum per label in `spectra.csv`.

## What the channel is

- **Brick wall at 4.6 kHz.** Every file has energy up to about 4.6 kHz and nothing above: the mean speech
  spectrum drops from -38 dB at 4 kHz to -104 dB at 5 kHz. The feed is
  band-limited by the radio system and the stream codec, not by the microphones. Anything the app expects
  above 4.6 kHz (fricatives s/f/th live at 4-8 kHz in natural speech) is simply not there.
- **Bass-heavy speech.** During speech the frequency below which 95 % of the energy lies is
  1594 Hz (median per file) and the spectral centroid 665 Hz.
  The mean speech spectrum peaks at 500 Hz and is already 10 dB down by 1 kHz and
  24 dB down by 3 kHz.
- **Levels.** Speech sits at -28 dBFS (median per file), the carrier-open floor at
  -48 dBFS: an in-channel SNR of about 20 dB. Squelch-closed gaps are digital
  silence (below -90 dBFS), so "silence" in the corpus is mostly the channel being closed, not room noise.
- **No mains hum** in the corpus (0.0 % of frames): the 60 Hz problem is only on the Jetson's USB mic.

## What is in the frames

| label | share of frames (mean per file) |
|---|---|
| speech | 58.7 % |
| silence | 28.2 % |
| other | 11.5 % |
| tone | 1.4 % |
| noise | 0.1 % |
| hum | 0.1 % |

- **Speech** is the bulk of every open carrier. "Other" is mostly unvoiced speech (consonants, breaths) and
  the first frames after a key-up.
- **Tones** are short. No file has a tone that holds still for 0.25 s or longer: there are no sustained
  sirens in these 58 calls, only brief alert/squelch beeps (the 1 kHz radio beep of CS010 and CS013 is one)
  and the odd vowel with one dominant harmonic. The files with the most tone frames are
  mocomdps-4390-1792804749 (6.9 %), 4395-1792803166 (6.5 %) and 4430-1792827672 (6.5 %).
- **Broadband noise** (static, engine, wind) is almost absent as separate frames: it is under the speech,
  not between it. The lowest-SNR files are mocomdps-4390-1792791761 (11 dB), 4430-1792827672 (15 dB) and
  4390-1792805285 (15 dB); listen to those for what "scratchy" is.

## How the synthetic voice compares

Piper's en_US-lessac-medium voice reading the STEMI script is darker than the radio: its 95 % roll-off is
719 Hz against the corpus' 1517 Hz, and it is full-band to 11 kHz. `scripts/corpus/radioize.py` equalises
a WAV to the corpus' mean speech spectrum (third-octave smoothed, +-24 dB), brick-walls it at 4.6 kHz,
soft-limits it and adds a -48 dBFS noise bed shaped like the carrier-open floor. On the STEMI script the
EQ is about -7 dB at 250 Hz, +3 dB at 2 kHz and -23 dB at 4 kHz; the result's roll-off is 1688 Hz. The
air test plays both versions through the speaker and mic (AIR_SCRIPTS_2026-10-26.md).

## Towards a cloned radio voice

What radioize.py does is the channel, not the person. The Piper voice is a placeholder: it is not natural
enough to judge the system by, and the air runs only show that test audio must be shaped like the channel.

**Whose voice.** The concern was raised that the paramedics in the corpus did not agree to be cloned; the
decision (2026-10-26) is to clone from the corpus for internal test audio only, never for anything a
listener could take as the person speaking. References are 12 s single-speaker cuts in `~/voice_refs/`
(medic_cs008_s025 = CS008 S025_01 at 69 s; medic_cs014_s015, medic_cs014_s054). A clip of one of us
reading a script works the same way for any service. The corpus also supplies the channel EQ, the brick
wall, the real between-words floor in `~/noise_bank/`, and later the key-up clicks and beeps.

**Services to compare** (`scripts/corpus/clone_voice_cloud.py`, keys in `~/.config/handoff-ai/secrets.env`):
ElevenLabs (instant voice clone, eleven_multilingual_v2), Cartesia (voice clone + sonic-3.6), Fish Audio
(zero-shot from the clip, s2.1-pro). Each reads the three scripts into `~/air_scripts/<script>_<service>.wav`
so `air_check.py --variant _<service>` plays them through the same speaker and mic. Local options for the
same clip: XTTS-v2 (`scripts/corpus/clone_voice.py`, ~/xtts-venv, CPU), and Fish's open OpenAudio-S1-mini,
F5-TTS or Chatterbox if a GPU box is available. Also worth a listen: a designed voice (ElevenLabs voice design
from a text description) rather than a clone, which needs no reference at all.

**Then the gold set.** With a voice that passes a listening test, generate many scripts (varied patients,
vitals, units, hospitals, wording) with known transcripts and known report fields, radioize them with the
corpus noise at the corpus' SNR, split into train/test by script, and score transcriber and report on the
test half; keep the real call-sets as the final check.

## Next cuts on the data

  need a re-decode. The first things to check by ear are the "other" frames and the short "tone" runs.
- The same survey on the Jetson mic recordings (`output/` live captures) shows what the dongle adds:
  the 60 Hz line and whatever room noise sits under the speech. Done 2026-10-27 on the one capture that
  survived (the air runs keep transcript and report but not the audio; `output/recording.wav` holds only
  the last take, the 23:05 STEMI clone through the dongle, 79 s): survey in `~/datasets/spectral_mic`.
  Speech 68.6 % of frames, noise floor -57.5 dBFS, SNR 21.8 dB (the corpus median: -47.8 dBFS floor,
  20.3 dB), hum 0 % of frames. Against the corpus mean spectra, two differences, both outside the voice band:
  below 125 Hz the mic path is 20 to 30 dB quieter than the feed (the 150 Hz high-pass of 254dc33 does
  its job; the feed's own floor is bassy), and above 4.5 kHz, where the feed falls off a cliff to -118 dB,
  the mic path holds a flat floor near -42 dB relative, 30 dB under the speech peak, from 4 to 8 kHz. The
  source audio was band-limited, so that shelf is the dongle and the room. A model trained on radio never
  sees energy there; for speaker-to-mic test runs a 4.6 kHz low-pass on the capture would make the
  audio match the corpus, but for a medic speaking straight into the mic that band is real speech
  (fricatives) and should stay. The "other" share is 29 % against the corpus' 10 %, which is the
  clone's voice and the room mixing, not a label to re-cut. Next: the air runner should copy the capture
  into its run folder so the next survey has more than one file.
