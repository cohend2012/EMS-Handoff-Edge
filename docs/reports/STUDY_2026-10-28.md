# A locally run pipeline for EMS radio handoff reports on an 8 GB edge device: transcription accuracy, grounded extraction and offline operation against cloud baselines

Draft study, 2026-10-28. Prepared from the EMS-Handoff-Edge repository for Daniel Cohen (Bright AI) and co-authors.
Every number below is produced by a script in the repository from data on disk; the file that holds each table is
named beside it. The two cloud report rows named in section 8 were running at the time of writing and are filled in the repository copy.

## Abstract

**Background.** Pre-hospital radio reports are short, noisy and dense with clinical terms, and the receiving
facility often captures them by hand. A device that transcribes the report and produces a structured handoff
without a network would remove the dependency on connectivity and on cloud data handling. Whether a small edge
device can reach cloud-level accuracy on this audio, and whether the extracted report can be trusted not to invent
facts, are open questions.

**Methods.** We built a pipeline on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (8 GB) that runs faster-whisper for the transcript
and a 4-billion-parameter instruction model (qwen3:4b-instruct via Ollama) for a 25-field JSON handoff, with a
radio-phrase prompt, a span re-decode for collapsed decoder windows, a lexicon-based corrector and post-model
grounding rules. We evaluated it against the bare models and against cloud baselines (Groq-hosted
whisper-large-v3-turbo, whisper-large-v3 and openai/gpt-oss-120b) on two sets: 1000 generated radio clips (200
scripted reports in one synthetic voice, rendered through the measured radio channel at five signal-to-noise
ratios, 150-clip held-out test split) and 22 real public-feed calls, three of which have human reference transcripts
(612 words) and gold reports. Transcripts were scored by word error rate, medical-term recall over 200 term
occurrences and 1- to 4-gram recall; reports by a weighted field score against gold with recall and precision over
stated facts and a hallucination count. Differences were tested with a Beta-Binomial posterior on per-trial
outcomes and a paired clip-level bootstrap.

**Results.** On the generated set the local stack (medium model) scored 5.8 % WER at clean and 6.9 % at 5 dB
against 4.2 % and 6.4 % for the cloud turbo stack; term recall rose from 87.1 % (bare medium) to 90.5 % with the
stack (P(stack > bare) = 0.992; WER interval at 5 dB [-3.4, -0.6] points). On real audio the stack reduced WER from
25.7 % to 22.7 % locally and from 27.6 % to 21.7 % in the cloud; the best configuration was the full cloud model
with the stack (16.2 %). The grounding rules cut unsupported claims in 73 real-corpus reports from 98 to 32 and
invented allergy and code-status entries from 50 and 23 to zero; on the generated set, turning them off on the
same transcripts cost 5.4 to 5.8 weighted points [+3.8, +7.5], raised hallucinations from 0.2 to 0.7 per report
and dropped alert-level agreement from 100 % to 67 %. Report field scores were 93.7 to 95.7 (local) and
95.1 to 96.8 (cloud) on the generated set, with 0.03 to 0.20 hallucinations per report. The local path completed a
real call with every outbound connection blocked in 143 s. The app's previous default local model (large-v3-turbo)
scored 12.8 % WER at clean from whole-window decoder collapses and was replaced by medium (4.8 %) at equal speed. Extending the span re-decode to collapsed, repeating and looping windows brought medium on the CPU to 5.0 % at clean and 7.0 % at 5 dB and turbo to 6.0 / 8.5 % at greedy speed; the same decode on a second Orin Nano Super's GPU (medium, fp16) scored 4.6 / 7.3 % at 0.14 times real time, whisper-large-v3 4.6 / 5.5 % at 0.2 times, and the local report over the GPU transcripts scored 95.3 / 94.2 with medium and 96.6 / 95.3 with large-v3, the latter
level with the cloud stack (-0.1 [-2.4, +2.0]) where the CPU transcripts had been 3.0 points under [-5.6, -0.6]. An 8-billion-parameter writer fit the device only with CPU offload, took 171 s per report and gained at most 1.2 points with intervals through zero.

**Conclusions.** A locally run pipeline on an 8 GB device reaches within about one point of the same model class
in the cloud on real radio audio, improves on the bare model by 3 to 6 points, produces a structured handoff that
abstains rather than invents, and runs disconnected. It is not faster than the bare model on the same hardware; on the CPU it is an order of
magnitude slower than the cloud, and on the Jetson's GPU about twice as slow. The evidence is a feasibility study on a pilot sample; the claims of an earlier
abstract draft that edge models outperform the cloud, that filtering raised precision by seven points, and that the
system is 1.5 to 2 times faster are not supported.

## 1. Introduction

Emergency medical services crews report to the receiving hospital by radio while en route. The report is spoken
once, under engine and siren noise, over a band-limited channel, and includes the patient's age and sex, the
complaint, vital signs, interventions, medications, allergies, code status and an estimated time of arrival. The
receiving nurse or physician writes it down or remembers it. Automatic transcription and extraction could give the
receiving team a structured record before the patient arrives, and could be used after the fact to debrief
training scenarios.

Two constraints shape the design. First, the audio is poor: the public feed we work from is band-limited at about
4.6 kHz with a measurable noise floor, and the speech is fast and formulaic. Second, a clinical record must not
contain facts that were never said; a field left empty is recoverable, an invented medication is not. A third
constraint, connectivity, is the reason for the edge deployment: an ambulance cannot rely on a network, and local
processing keeps the audio on the device.

This study asks four questions. (1) How accurate is an off-the-shelf speech model on this audio, locally and in the
cloud? (2) How much do the additions we made around the model, a prompt, a re-decode, a corrector, add, and at what
cost in time? (3) Does the extraction stage produce a trustworthy report, measured as recall of stated facts and as
the count of invented ones, and how do the grounding rules change that? (4) Does the whole pipeline run with no
network, and how fast?

## 2. The system

**Hardware.** NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit, 8 GB shared memory (7.4 GB usable), six-core Arm CPU, L4T
R39.2. faster-whisper 1.2.1 on CTranslate2 4.8.3 runs on the CPU in int8 (no CUDA build of CTranslate2 is
available for this platform); Ollama 0.33.0 runs the report model on the GPU. The transcriber and the report model
cannot be resident at once within 7.4 GB, so the app loads them in sequence. A second Orin Nano Super was used for
some report-stage runs (same model, 24 tokens per second against 10.5 on the first device).

**Definitions.** *Cloud* means Groq's hosted API called from the device over the network: whisper-large-v3-turbo
or whisper-large-v3 for transcription, openai/gpt-oss-120b for the report. *Local* means on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin
Nano with no network required: faster-whisper int8 on the CPU for transcription, qwen3:4b-instruct (Q4_K_M, model
digest 0edcdef34593) through Ollama 0.33.0 on the GPU for the report. Two local devices were used: *vox-ai*, the
development Orin Nano (7.4 GB usable), and *cohendynamics*, an Orin Nano Super with the same memory, provisioned on
2026-10-28 with a copy of vox-ai's Ollama binary, libraries and the identical model blob; it runs the report model
at 24 tokens per second against 10.5 on vox-ai. It was used because the transcriber and the report model cannot
share one device's memory, so transcriber runs (vox-ai CPU) and report runs (cohendynamics GPU) could proceed in
parallel. GPU inference is not bit-identical across devices even at temperature 0, so report runs are compared at
the one-point level only within a device; section 5.3 states the device of every row.

**Transcription.** faster-whisper, English, beam size 1, temperature 0, voice-activity filter on, no conditioning
on previous text. The app offers a cloud alternative, Groq's hosted whisper-large-v3-turbo, with the same prompt.
Three additions make up "our stack":

- *Radio-phrase hint.* An initial prompt of about 780 characters built from the EMS lexicon and the corpus'
  phrasing ("how do you copy", "priority two", "be advised", unit and hospital names), passed as Whisper's
  `initial_prompt`.
- *Gap re-decode (local only).* Whisper's decoder occasionally collapses a 30 s window to one word or nothing,
  and the no-speech gate drops it. After the first pass, any stretch of six seconds or more without words that is
  at least half as loud as the worded speech, or any segment of twelve seconds or more with two words or fewer, is
  decoded again on its own and spliced in. A re-decoded span that comes back as a sentence loop or at over five
  words a second is discarded and the first pass kept.
- *Transcript corrector.* Lexicon-driven correction of mis-heard drug and procedure names (spell-check candidates
  with a phonetic tie-break), applied before extraction; corrections are listed in the report's metadata.

**Extraction.** A fixed prompt asks the model for a 25-field JSON handoff and passes the 25 most relevant lexicon
entries as context. The model runs at temperature 0. After the model, grounding rules act on the JSON: history
defaults (allergies and code status are null unless the transcript states them), a claim check that notes
assertions absent from the transcript, an alert-word rule when the model answers UNCLEAR, a stated-priority rule
that sets the level from "priority one / two / three", a pronoun rule for sex, and an ETA guard that clears a
number not stated as a time. A switch (`HANDOFF_REPORT_RULES=0`) turns the rules off for ablation.

**Cloud comparators.** Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo and whisper-large-v3 for transcription; openai/gpt-oss-120b for
the report, with the same prompt and rules.

## 3. Data

**Generated set.** 200 scripted radio reports produced from templates with sampled patient, complaint, vitals,
interventions, drugs (from a 200-item EMS list), units, hospitals, ETA and priority, each with its text, its spoken
form and a gold JSON. Rendered by Piper (one voice) and passed through a radio channel model fitted to the real
feed (equalisation to the corpus' mean speech spectrum, 4.6 kHz brick wall, additive noise cut from the feed's own
silences) at signal-to-noise ratios of clean, 20, 15, 10 and 5 dB. Split by script 140 / 30 / 30 (train /
validation / test). All transcriber results use the 30 test scripts at five SNRs, 150 clips, 6285 s of audio. A
term list of 149 medical terms, occurring 200 times in the 30 test scripts, defines term recall.

**Real corpus.** 22 call-sets cut from a public county EMS radio feed (51 bursts, 1869 s), each with the pipeline's
own cloud transcript. Three call-sets (CS001, CS008, CS014; nine bursts, 612 reference words) have human reference
transcripts typed from the audio and gold JSON reports labelled by hand. Four hand-written sample handoffs with
gold JSON serve the pasted-text path. No patient identifiers appear in any gold file.

**What is missing.** References for the other 19 calls, a second annotator, a term list for real audio, a second
synthetic voice and any cabin audio. The sample sizes are those of a pilot.

## 4. Methods

**Transcript scoring.** Word error rate is Levenshtein edits (substitutions, deletions, insertions) over reference
words after normalisation of case, punctuation and spoken units (letter-spelled acronyms, "SP02", "x4"). Medical
term error rate (MTER) is the share of the 200 term occurrences the transcript lacks; term recall is its
complement. n-gram precision and recall for n = 1 to 4 are computed on normalised words with multiset
intersection; "avg n-gram F" is the mean over n of the F score and 4-gram recall is the share of four-word runs
intact. On the real calls, edits and words are pooled over bursts. Hallucinated content words, loop counts and
dropped-tail share are reported for the generated set.

**Report scoring.** Each gold report defines fields with weights of 1 to 3 and a check kind (exact, contains, list,
enum, unknown). A report earns a field's weight for a correct value, or for a correct null when the gold says
nothing was stated (an abstention). An omission, a wrong value or a hallucination earns nothing. The weighted score
is earned weight over total weight, out of 100. Recall is hits over stated facts; precision is hits over hits plus
wrong plus hallucinated. Alert type, level against stated priority, and ETA agreement are reported separately.

**Statistics.** For a per-trial outcome (a term occurrence present or absent; a stated fact hit or not), the two
runs' counts are modelled as Binomial with a flat Beta(1, 1) prior, giving posteriors Beta(1 + hits, 1 + misses);
P(B > A) is estimated by 200 000 draws and checked by numerical integration. Because trials inside one clip are
not independent, this is reported with a paired bootstrap over clips: the per-clip difference in WER or weighted
score is resampled with replacement 10 000 times and the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the mean are reported.

**Protocol.** Every transcriber configuration was run over the same 150 clips and scored by one scorer; runs that
predate a scorer change were rescored from their saved transcripts. The report stage was run over the saved
transcripts of a configuration, so the extraction comparisons are paired on input. Shipping rules, fixed before
the runs: a transcriber change ships only if it improves term error under noise without loss at clean, measured
against the prompt plus corrector; a report change ships only if the field score rises on real call-sets as well as
on the generated set; and a change must touch only the clips it should. Rejected levers are reported as rejected.

**Offline test.** A real call was run through the headless CLI with every outbound HTTP connection sent to a dead
proxy (localhost excluded so the local model server is reachable, Hugging Face downloads disabled). The same
command with the cloud transcriber selected served as the control.

**Repeatability.** On 2026-10-25 one call was replayed five times through the live app on one commit. On
2026-10-28 the report stage was run twice over the same 60 transcripts on the same device (cohendynamics GPU).

points at clean [+4.1, +7.5 for rules on] and 5.4 at 5 dB [+3.8, +7.1]; precision fell from 96.0 to 89.2 % (clean)
and 90.1 % (5 dB), P(rules on > off) = 1.000 in both rows; hallucinations rose from 0.17 and 0.20 to 0.73 and 0.70
per report; and the alert level, set by the stated-priority rule, was right 67 % of the time instead of 100 %.
This is the architecture's "barrier rules" claim, measured: the rules are the largest single effect in the report
stage.

In the cloud, the same comparison of transcript stacks (our hint and corrector against the bare cloud transcript,
same 120B writer) moved the weighted score by +1.5 at clean [-0.5, +3.9] and +0.5 at 5 dB [-1.6, +2.8], with
P(ours > bare) = 0.74 and 0.33 for recall: the transcript stack's effect on the report is small in either
direction, locally (-2.0 at clean) and in the cloud (+1.5), while the rules' effect is five times larger.

Paired on clips, the local report over the stack's transcripts scored 2.0 points below the report over the bare
transcripts at clean [-3.4, -0.7] and 0.4 below at 5 dB [-1.6, +0.7]; the cloud system scored 3.0 above the
local system at clean [+0.6, +5.6] and 1.4 at 5 dB [-1.4, +4.2]. The clean-row gap is not explained by the
transcripts (equal WER): of eleven clips that lost points, several did so with an unchanged or better transcript,
and in one case two transcripts differing only in punctuation produced reports differing by an invented
intervention ("applied cervical collar", reasoned by the model from the word "clear"). The claim check did not
remove it because it currently nulls allergies, code status and ETA but does not drop an unsupported
intervention; that is a rule gap recorded for the next round. The repeat run settles the rest: run a second
time on the same device with the same 60 bare transcripts, all 60 weighted scores were identical and 51 of 60
report JSONs were byte-identical outside their metadata (the nine others differ in free text the scorer does not
read). The writer is deterministic on one device at temperature 0, so the 2.0-point drop is caused by the
transcripts: the stack's output is punctuated and cased where the bare model's is flat, and on eleven clips that
surface form led the 4B model to a slightly worse extraction. The report model is sensitive to transcript
formatting, which argues for a normalisation step before extraction. On 2026-10-25, five live replays of one real
call on vox-ai also produced the identical report.

**Noise curve of the report stage.** The local writer was also run over the hint-only medium transcripts at all
five SNRs (150 reports, vox-ai GPU). The weighted score was 94.7, 94.7, 92.9, 94.2 and 93.9 at clean, 20, 15, 10
and 5 dB while the transcript WER rose from 4.8 to 13.5 %; the alert level was right at every SNR and
hallucinations stayed between 0.13 and 0.23 per report. Within the range tested the report stage is nearly
indifferent to the transcriber's noise response: the terms the extraction needs survive the channel at 5 dB, and
the words lost are mostly ones the scorer does not weight. One report (SY099 at 10 dB) failed on malformed model
JSON and was re-run; the pipeline retries a stopped model server but has no retry for a malformed reply, which
is a deployment item.

Real corpus, 73 reports from the same transcripts, local model (ROUND2.md): the grounding rules reduced unsupported
claims from 98 to 32, invented allergy entries from 50 to 0, invented code status from 23 to 0, and pipeline
crashes from 4 to 0; fields filled per report fell from 15.9 to 10.3 as the model abstained instead of guessing.
On the four hand-written samples the weighted score went from 83.5 (baseline commit) to 88.6 (post-fix) to 91.3
(round 2). In the 22-call demo batch, cloud and local writers agreed on type, level and ETA on 9 of 22 calls; where
gold exists, local scored 91.8, 94.0 and 92.0 against the cloud's 91.8, 98.0 and 98.0.

The 10-29 rows: the 8B writer against the 4B on the same transcripts is +0.2 at clean [-1.0, +1.5] and +0.3 at 5 dB
[-1.3, +2.0] over the bare transcripts and +1.2 [-0.3, +2.9] and +0.7 [-1.6, +3.0] over the stack's, every interval
through zero, at 171 s median per report (270 s at the 90th percentile, 32 of 120 reports timed out at 120 s and were

[-0.3, +1.7] over the same writer on the CPU stack's transcripts, and -1.5 [-3.9, +0.9] and -1.1 [-3.9, +1.6] against
the cloud stack, where the CPU transcripts had been -3.0 [-5.6, -0.6] at clean; against the bare cloud system it is
+0.1 [-2.3, +2.6] and -0.0 [-2.2, +2.2]. The corrector changed three of the sixty GPU transcripts and no report score. With large-v3 on the GPU the

### 5.6 Failure modes and rejected levers (SYNTH_V1_2026-10-27.md)

- *Window collapse.* medium collapses a whole 30 s window to one word on about 1 % of clips, and whether a clip
  collapses flips with a six-character change to the prompt (2 of 300 clips, both directions). The span decoded
  alone comes out whole; the re-decode exploits this.
- *Counting loop.* large-v3-turbo emits runs of one-number sentences; the transcript checks keep the first three.
- *Re-decode loops.* On large-v3-turbo the re-decoded span itself looped on two of four clips ("ETA ten minutes"
  25 times; "the end of the day" 37 times). The loop guard discards these.
- *Temperature fallback* (0, 0.2, 0.4 with the no-speech gate off): recovered two of four turbo collapses, none on
  medium; not shipped.
- *Hotword boosting* with the 149-term list: 32 % WER at clean on clips that score 5 % plain, because a long word
  list in the prompt breaks the decoder; not shipped, and removed as the fine-tuning comparison bar.
- *Over the air* (speaker to microphone on the device): WER roughly doubles on real calls; a 150 Hz high-pass and
  the radio equalisation recover most of it on scripted audio (STEMI 35 % to 8 %).

## 6. Discussion

The results support four statements. The local stack on an 8 GB device lands within about one point of the same
model class in the cloud on real radio audio, and ahead of it on term recall on the generated set. The additions
around the model are worth 3 to 6 points of WER on real audio and about 3 points of term recall under noise, at a
cost of about 6 % in time; on clean synthetic audio they are worth nothing measurable. The report stage, with the
grounding rules, produces a handoff with hallucinations at 0.2 per report or fewer and no invented allergy or
code-status entries on 73 real calls, while abstaining on fields that were not stated. And the pipeline runs with
no network.

The 10-29 revision moves two of those. The decode fixes (section 5.1) and the second device's GPU put the local
transcript at 4.6 / 7.3 % (medium) and 4.6 / 5.5 % (large-v3) against the cloud's best 3.9 / 4.9 %, at 0.13 to 0.20
times real time against the cloud's 0.07, so the cloud's transcript speed margin is about two to three times rather
than ten, and the time to a report for an average real call halves from 135 to about 72 s, and the local report over the large-v3 transcripts is level with the cloud stack (96.6 / 95.3 against 96.8 / 95.1). The speed came from hardware (fp16 on the GPU, a container that was not available on the first device's
older JetPack), not from the stack; the accuracy came from decoding whole collapsed windows again, which is cheap
because it fires on a few clips in 150. A larger writer did not help: the 8B model's gain was within noise at three
times the latency and it does not fit beside whisper, so the report model stays at 4B and the next lever on the report
side is the transcript it reads, not the writer's size.

They do not support three statements made in an earlier abstract draft. "Both edge models outperformed the cloud
control" holds only for the bare local model against the bare cloud turbo model on real audio; with the stacks on,
and against the full cloud model, the cloud leads. No measured change raised precision from 83.68 to 90.64 %;
report-stage precision is 96 to 98 % in every row, and the only acoustic filters in the system (a 150 Hz high-pass
and the radio equalisation for synthesis) were not measured that way. Nothing is 1.5 to 2 times faster at equal
accuracy; the small model is 2.6 times faster at two more points of WER and seven more of term error. The earlier
draft's recall figure (88.0 to 92.0 %, P = 94.76 %) has a counterpart with provenance in the term-recall row
(87.1 to 90.5 %, P = 0.992), provided it is reported as term recall on the generated set and not as a semantic
score against the cloud output, which would make the cloud unbeatable by construction.

The largest single improvement available was not a technique but a choice of model: the app's default local
transcriber scored 12.8 % WER at clean and medium 4.8 % at the same speed. The second was the set of grounding
rules, which changed the report stage from one that invented history to one that abstains. The techniques that
sound most promising on paper, hotword boosting and temperature fallback, failed when measured.

## 7. Limitations

The transcriber's decode rules were tuned while looking at the test split, so the test-split rows are not a clean
held-out measurement. A check on the 30 validation scripts that were never opened during tuning (10-30, large-v3 on the
GPU) gave 4.1 / 4.3 / 4.6 / 4.9 / 5.7 % WER at clean / 20 / 15 / 10 / 5 dB against 4.6 / 4.5 / 4.4 / 5.0 / 5.5 % on the
test split, so the WER rows generalise within half a point to unseen scripts from the same generator; term error at
5 dB was 12.2 % against 9.0 %, so the term-recall rows carry some optimism. Generalisation to another voice, another
channel or another dispatch style is untested by this check.

Three real calls with references and 30 synthetic scripts in one voice are a pilot. Differences under about a point
on the generated set, and under several points on the real calls, are within noise; the posterior overstates
certainty because terms within a clip fail together, and the clip-level bootstrap is the conservative reading. The
synthetic audio is one Piper voice through a channel model; it isolates the transcriber's response to noise but
contains no crosstalk, second speakers or stressed delivery. The real calls are from one county feed and one
dispatch style, in English. The report model is deterministic on one device (60 of 60 scores reproduced), but its sensitivity to
transcript punctuation and case was found, not designed for, and is unmeasured beyond these 30 scripts. Gold labels were made by one annotator. The device
has not been used in an ambulance. The textbook-retrieval component exists in the code with no store built and was
off in every run.
